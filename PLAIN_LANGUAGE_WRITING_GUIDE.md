- TODO: Give the technical term after the plain explanation, not before it.

- TODO: State what the analysis cannot prove whenever a result could be mistaken for observed animal movement.

## Terms used in this project

- **Core:** a large area supported by the historical cheetah evidence and treated as a starting or ending area in the model.
- **Resistance:** the assumed difficulty of moving through a map cell. Higher resistance means harder modeled movement.
- **Least-cost path:** the single route with the lowest total resistance between two cores.
- **Circuitscape current:** modeled flow spread across all possible routes between cores. It is not electricity in the landscape and not a count of cheetahs.
- **Effective resistance:** one number describing the overall modeled difficulty of connecting two cores when all possible routes are considered.
- **Structural connectivity:** how well the shape and condition of the landscape appear to connect places. It does not prove animals used the connection.
- **Edge betweenness:** how often one link is part of the shortest network connection between other cores. A higher value means the link is more important to the structure of the modeled network.
- **KNN:** a rule connecting each core to its nearest neighbors.
- **MST:** the smallest set of links that keeps the whole core network connected.
- **KNN+MST:** a link selected by both rules.
- **Bridge:** a link whose removal splits the network into separate pieces.
- **Percentile:** a way to rank cells. The 90th percentile contains values higher than about 90 percent of the comparison cells.
- **Jaccard overlap:** the shared area divided by the total area covered by either map. Zero means no overlap and one means perfect overlap.
- **Sensitivity analysis:** repeating the model with reasonable alternative assumptions to see which conclusions change.
- **Plausibility check:** a limited comparison asking whether results appear reasonable. It is weaker than independent validation.
