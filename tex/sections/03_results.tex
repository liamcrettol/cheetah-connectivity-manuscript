\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Connectivity change, \studyperiod}

The modeled current concentration outside the cores was similar in 2012 and 2024, with a few small areas of change. Modeled current in 2024 is mapped in \figref{fig:current2024}, and its change since 2012 in \figref{fig:change}. The median change in modeled cost between cores was $-1.224$ percent, with 92 of all 253 core pairs becoming harder to move between and 161 becoming easier. The largest increase was +13.1 percent, for the link between cores 33 and 38. The overlap of the highest-current areas between successive snapshots, and between 2012 and 2024, was 0.91 to 0.93, where 1 would mean perfect overlap.

Of the 45 selected links, 18 became harder and 27 became easier. The overall pattern of change was not meaningfully related to baseline betweenness ($\rho \approx 0.01$), so the temporal-change hypothesis was mixed rather than supported. The 18 links that became harder did not have higher betweenness than the 27 that became easier (Mann--Whitney one-sided $p = 0.36$), and among the 18 harder links, the bigger losses did not fall on the more important links ($\rho = -0.22$, not significant). However, the losses were fairly concentrated, with the five hardest-hit of the 18 links carrying 46 percent of the total increase.

\subsection{Priority links and sensitivity checks}

Thirty-two of the 45 selected links passed the sensitivity tests and remained in the main priority group. The other 13 links were kept as uncertain links. The weighting test kept 34 links, and the fence test removed two more. The vegetation test did not remove any additional links from the remaining group. The main group is therefore not a small handful of links. It contains most of the network, which only partly matches the original expectation that the priorities would be limited to a small subset, so the link-stability hypothesis was only partly supported. The modeled links are shown with transportation and fence context in \figref{fig:networkcontext}, and the 10 highest-ranked links are listed in Table~\ref{tab:toplinks}.

The modeled cost of the 32 priority links was fairly stable, but the exact line taken by the least-cost path was less stable, meaning that the modeled route could shift by more than 1 km in some years. Of 180 route-and-year comparisons, 49 had less than 80 percent overlap within 1 km. This affected 21 of the 32 priority links. The route between cores 17 and 24 had no overlap in 2020. The vegetation assumptions changed the size of the modeled cost change, but not its direction for 43 of 45 links; the median absolute change grew from about 3.6 to 9.3 percent as vegetation was given more weight. Allowing two, three, four, or five nearest neighbors created 31, 45, 61, or 77 links, and the highest-ranked links changed. Link 17--24 was the only bridge under the three-neighbor choice, which means that removing it would disconnect the network.

\subsection{Independent tracking check}
\label{sec:results_tracking}

Across 549 day-to-day moves, the location a cheetah actually reached had lower modeled resistance than most of the alternative moves it could have made (Table~\ref{tab:limpopo_steps}, panel A). On average, the real move had lower resistance than 56 percent of its alternatives (95 percent interval 51--61 percent), where 50 percent would indicate no difference, and seven of the nine animals showed this pattern. Averaged along the straight line between locations, the result was almost the same (55 percent). Modeled current showed no clear pattern: the real move had higher current than 53 percent of its alternatives, but the interval (47--59 percent) included 50, and only five of the nine animals leaned that way.

All nine resistance surfaces gave 55 to 56 percent (panel B). The five fence treatments gave identical values, because no mapped fence lies within 20~km of any track. Road density carried most of the result (panel C): on its own it reached 55 percent, and leaving it out reduced the main surface to 54 percent, with an interval that reached 50. Livestock, slope, and nighttime lights each showed a small lean of 51 to 52 percent. Built-up area barely varied in this district and could not separate real from alternative moves, and vegetation on its own leaned slightly in the opposite direction (48 percent, interval 43--52). Real moves crossed mapped roads less often than their alternatives (26 against 35 percent), and eight of the nine animals crossed fewer roads than expected (panel D).

In the broader comparison against random points across the tracked area (Table~\ref{tab:limpopo}), cheetah locations had lower modeled resistance 60 percent of the time (seven of nine animals) and higher modeled current 65 percent of the time (six of nine animals). Widening the available area by 20~km gave the same picture.

In the weaker, non-independent comparison using the range archive itself, occupied 10-km cells numbered 413 in 2010--2012 and 450 in 2013--2016, with an overlap score of 0.239. Later records fell in slightly higher modeled resistance, and a larger share fell inside the fixed cores. Almost no records were close to the priority paths (zero early cells and three later cells). No occurrence records were available after 2016, so this comparison cannot speak to the 2020 or 2024 results.

The monitoring-priority hypothesis was not tested, because the cell-level monitoring index it depended on was not built (Section~\ref{sec:mcda}).

\subsection{Protected-area coverage of priority connectivity}

Of the usable outside-core area, 31.26 percent was protected. In 2024, only 23.27 percent of the area above the 80th percentile of modeled current was protected, rising to 25.54 percent above the 90th percentile, 33.45 percent above the 95th, and 44.63 percent above the 99th (Table~\ref{tab:pa}). The same general pattern appeared in all four years; in 2012 the corresponding values were 24.23, 26.31, 35.57, and 44.17 percent. High-current land outside the cores was therefore less protected than the comparison area up to the 90th percentile and more protected above the 95th. Because the direction changed across thresholds, the protected-area-coverage hypothesis was not supported under its full rule.
